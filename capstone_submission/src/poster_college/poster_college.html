Silicon Sampling for Indian Public Opinion

Abstract / Introduction / Motivation

Silicon sampling gives a language model a respondent's demographic profile and treats its answer as that person's survey answer. If it were faithful, surveys would become far cheaper.

We test this in India on Pew Religion in India (29,999 adults) and WVS-7 India (1,692 adults). Every method is scored on the same respondent-question rows as matched classifiers, and we check direction, calibration and subgroup gaps, not only accuracy.

Literature Review / Background

  • US attitudes are reproduced (Argyle 2023) but many groups misrepresented (Santurkar 2023).
  • Individually, LLMs match or trail baselines and inflate group gaps (Chen 2026; Miranda 2025).
  • Personas explain under 10% of variance (Hu 2024); fine-tuning can generalise (Suh 2025).

Gap: India, the profile's own contribution, transfer to unseen questions.

Objectives

RQ1 How does a prompted LLM use the profile? RQ2 What does fine-tuning change versus classifiers? RQ3 Does it transfer to unseen questions? RQ4 Do size or more data matter?

Methods

Data. Pew fold 0: 23,999 training and 6,000 test respondents, 8 items, 46,209 test answers; items 9 to 16 held out for transfer.

Prompt. Profile, question and numbered options in one fixed template; a no-profile control measures what the profile adds.

Predictors. Zero-shot Qwen2.5-7B, the same model fine-tuned with QLoRA, and majority, logistic and gradient-boosting classifiers on the same rows.

Readout. The probability of each answer digit at the next token.

Methods (continued)

QLoRA fine-tuning. 4-bit NF4 base, LoRA rank 16 and alpha 32 on the attention projections, 10.1M trainable parameters, 184,853 examples, 5,777 steps, 6.6 hours on a university lab GPU.

Scoring. Kaggle 2 x T4 in fp16, about 0.2 s per answer and 75 minutes per condition. Classifiers train in under a minute on a laptop.

Reproducibility. Code, folds and prompts are in the project repository, and every figure on this poster is computed from stored model outputs.

Diagnostics

  • Accuracy and negative log-likelihood
  • Expected calibration error
  • Profile share of prediction variance
  • Directional validity: correlation and slope of group differences
  • Jensen-Shannon divergence
  • Subgroup gaps, Holm-corrected permutation tests
  • Respondent-clustered bootstrap intervals
  • Per-question and per-subgroup breakdowns

Lessons from the build

  • WVS region labels were wrong on every code until checked against the official annex; fixing it moved subgroup gaps by several points.
  • The first prompt wording caused 13% refusals; the final wording gave 0%.
  • An apparent collapse on one item was a decoding artefact, fixed by the next-digit readout.
  • All reported numbers come from one training run; more seeds are future work.

Results & Discussion

On Pew fold 0 (46,209 answers) the fine-tuned model reaches 38.0%, against 30.4% for always guessing the majority answer, 38.5% for logistic regression and 40.0% for gradient boosting. Zero-shot Qwen2.5-7B scores 16.1%.

Fine-tuning closes 79% of the gap from the majority guess to gradient boosting, but stays 2.1 points below it.

Per question, the fine-tuned model beats the majority guess on all 8 items, by 1.3 to 24.8 points, and trails gradient boosting on every one.

Calibration and confidence

Calibration error is 0.009: when the model says 60%, it is right about 60% of the time.

Above 70% confidence it is right 87.3% of the time, on 1,338 answers, nearly all from the number-of-children question. The driver is marital status: unmarried respondents are 98.8% confident and 96.6% accurate.

A classifier finds the same rule, so this shows a learned pattern, not superiority. The model is strongest where the profile decides the answer and weakest on opinions.

Profile use and transfer

Zero-shot, the profile explains 29% of the model's prediction variance against 4% for real answers, yet removing it raises accuracy by 3.6 points: the model reacts strongly and in the wrong direction.

Group differences correlate 0.22 (zero-shot), 0.58 (fine-tuned) and 0.64 (gradient boosting) with the truth.

On 8 unseen items the adapter scores 28.9% against 28.5% for the base model: no detectable transfer.

Discussion

Prompting alone does not make a faithful Indian respondent. Fine-tuning gives a calibrated simulator close to, but not above, matched classifiers, at far higher cost.

Limits. One fold, one seed, one epoch; a small transfer test; survey weights not applied.

Next. All five folds and seeds, more held-out items, larger models on the same rows.
