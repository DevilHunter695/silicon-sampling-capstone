1. ABSTRACT / INTRODUCTION / MOTIVATION

Silicon sampling asks a language model to answer a survey as a respondent with a given demographic profile. It would be far cheaper than fielding a questionnaire. But is it faithful?

We test it in India on Pew Religion in India (N = 29,999) and WVS-7 India (N = 1,692), scoring every method on the same respondent-item rows as matched classifiers, and checking direction, calibration and subgroup gaps, not only accuracy.

2. LITERATURE REVIEW / BACKGROUND

  • LLMs reproduce US demographic-attitude correlations (Argyle 2023) but misrepresent many groups (Santurkar 2023).
  • At the individual level they match or trail baselines and inflate gaps between groups (Chen 2026; Miranda 2025).
  • Persona variables explain under 10% of variance (Hu 2024).
  • Fine-tuning generalises to unseen subpopulations (Suh 2025).

Gap: India, what the profile itself contributes, and transfer to unseen questions.

3. OBJECTIVES

  • RQ1 How does a prompted LLM use the profile: how much, and in which direction?
  • RQ2 What does fine-tuning change versus zero-shot and matched classifiers?
  • RQ3 Does the adaptation transfer to unseen questions?
  • RQ4 Do a larger model or more labelled data change the picture?

4. METHODS

Same prompt and identical rows for every predictor: zero-shot Qwen2.5-7B (chat, raw, no profile), QLoRA fine-tuned Qwen2.5-7B, larger LLMs, and majority / logistic / GBM baselines.
  • Data split: 8 Pew items, five respondent-level folds; fold 0 = 23,999 train / 6,000 test respondents (46,209 test answers). Items 9 to 16 held out for transfer.
  • Readout: the probability of each answer digit at the next token, so every method gives a full distribution, not just a label.
  • QLoRA: 4-bit, rank 16, 10.1M trainable parameters, 184,853 examples, 6.6 GPU-hours.
  • Diagnostics: accuracy, NLL, ECE, variance share, directional validity, JSD, Holm-corrected subgroup gaps.
  • Compute: about 0.2 s per answer on a T4 GPU; classifiers are far cheaper.

6. CONCLUSION & FUTURE SCOPE

  • A prompted 7B model is not a faithful Indian respondent: it reacts to the profile strongly, in the wrong direction.
  • QLoRA fine-tuning gives a calibrated simulator close to, but not above, matched classifiers.
  • Next: all five folds and seeds, more held-out items, survey weights, and larger models on the same rows.
