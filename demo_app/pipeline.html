05 / the verdict

What works, and what does not

Both lists come from the same run. We show the failures on purpose.

Works

Fine-tuning fixes a broken zero-shot model

From 16.1% to 38.0% accuracy, with negative log-likelihood falling from 4.26 to 1.48.

zero-shot 16.1% → fine-tuned 38.0%
Its probabilities can be trusted

Calibration error of 0.009, in line with the classifiers. When it is at least 90% sure, it is right 97.8% of the time.

ECE 0.009 · 846 answers at 90%+ confidence
Questions the profile can answer

Number of children: 56.4% against 31.6% for the majority guess. Unmarried respondents are almost always predicted correctly.

unmarried: 96.6% correct, 98.8% predicted with 70%+ confidence
It gets group differences in the right direction

Predicted gaps between groups correlate 0.58 with the real gaps on the 200-respondent subset where zero-shot reaches only 0.22, and 0.82 on the full fold.

directional r: zero-shot 0.22 · fine-tuned 0.58 (subset), 0.82 (full)

Does not work

Prompting alone

The zero-shot model reacts strongly to the profile but in the wrong direction. Removing the profile raises its accuracy by 3.6 points.

profile share of variance: model 29%, real answers 4%
Beating a simple classifier

Gradient boosting on the same rows reaches 40.0% and leads on every question, by 1.2 to 2.7 points, in under a minute of CPU time.

GBM 40.0% · fine-tuned 38.0%
Opinion questions

On politicians in religion the model is barely above the majority guess. Opinions are hard to read from nine demographics.

36.7% vs 35.4% majority
Transfer to unseen questions

No detectable gain on 8 held-out items, using an approximate port of the adapter. Too small to rule transfer out.

28.9% vs 28.5% · p = 0.18
